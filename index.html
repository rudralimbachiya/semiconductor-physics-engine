Semiconductor Mechanics & Band Theory

Model: The engine uses temperature-dependent band gap, intrinsic carrier concentration, charge neutrality, Fermi–Dirac carrier statistics with simplified shallow-impurity ionization, electric field from device geometry, and low-field drift transport. The animated carriers are visual samples and are not individual microscopic trajectories.

Drift direction: For a given electric field, conduction-band electrons drift opposite to the field because they are negatively charged; holes drift in the field direction because they behave as positive charge carriers. The two carrier motions therefore contribute to conventional current in the same direction.

The Forbidden Gap: In a crystalline solid, electrons act as waves diffracted by the periodic lattice. At specific energy levels, destructive Bragg interference occurs, canceling out the electron waves. Because no valid quantum state (solution to the Schrödinger equation) exists in this region, electrons cannot occupy it.

5. Charge Neutrality

n + NA− = p + ND+

The equilibrium solver enforces charge neutrality using Fermi–Dirac statistics and simplified donor/acceptor ionization. This is still a bulk educational model, not a self-consistent TCAD solution.

Relevant Governing Equations

1. Varshni Equation (Temperature-Dependent Band Gap)

Eg(T) = Eg(0) - [ (αT2) / (T + β) ]

The engine uses this empirical relation to calculate the band gap at the selected lattice temperature. Material-specific α and β parameters are stored in the material model.
2. Planck-Einstein Relation (Band-to-Band Photon)

λ = hc / E ≈ 1240 / Eg

For the simplified radiative-recombination model, the emitted photon energy is taken as approximately the band gap. This is an idealization; indirect-gap materials such as silicon are inefficient light emitters.
3. Fermi–Dirac Carrier Statistics

n = NC F1/2[(EF - EC)/(kBT)]    and    p = NV F1/2[(EV - EF)/(kBT)]

The engine numerically evaluates a normalized Fermi–Dirac F1/2 integral and solves charge neutrality with simplified donor/acceptor ionization. The Maxwell–Boltzmann expression is recovered only in the non-degenerate limit.
4. Low-Field Drift Current Density

J = qnμeE + qpμhE

The engine calculates J from the carrier concentrations, temperature-adjusted mobilities, and electric field. Device current is then I = J A using the defined device cross-section.
